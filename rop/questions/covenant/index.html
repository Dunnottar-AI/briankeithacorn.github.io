What is a covenant?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“It this new covenant is about grace, not justice. Second, it will have the power to change hearts. This new covenant will have the power to transform us and make us like what we long to be. telling people what to do isn't enough. We need transforming grace if we are ever to change. And that's what the new covenant promises. This is God's answer to the problem of sin. A different way of relating forgiveness instead of works, grace instead of law. And the question, the only question that matters is which covenant are you under?”

Brett McNeill · The New Covenant · Esther 8:3-10 · Nov 17, 2025 · Watch this part (16:30) →

“And so Satan's claim on them evaporated like that. What he intended for evil, God intended for our salvation. And through all of this, Jesus was able to make a new covenant, a different covenant, one that would not mean death for his people, but life. what we call the covenant of grace, which says that all who believe in Jesus, who repent of their sins and surrender to him, will not face the eternal judgment and wrath that they deserve when they die. But they will enter into his heavenly kingdom as if they had been the ones who obeyed, not Jesus.”

Brett McNeill · The Gospel According to Esther · Esther 10:1-3 · Dec 29, 2025 · Watch this part (18:01) →

“Why didn't he usher in the eternal state and judge the world that day? What is he doing in the meantime? And that's what we want we want to look at in chapter 8 of Esther this morning. Last week, we looked at that center section of this chapter and the decree that Mori sent out in the king's name as a picture of the new covenant that Jesus inaugurated through his death and his resurrection. That the new covenant is that full revelation of the covenant of grace where we are not judged by how good we are but how good Jesus is.”

Brett McNeill · The Gracious Victor · Esther 8:1-2, 10-17 · Nov 24, 2025 · Watch this part (4:48) →

“And so in the days of exile, the prophet Jeremiah picked up on that imagery when he announced the new covenant. He foretold both the coming exile but also God's rescue that would follow the exile. And he said that God would make a new covenant with Israel, not like the one that he made through Moses at Mount Si, a law covenant that couldn't change people's hearts, that he would make instead a different kind, one of grace that could give them new hearts and cleanse them of their sins and bring forgiveness. He would rescue them not with the law, but with grace. And in describing that in Jeremiah 31, he said he would make adulterous Israel a virgin once again.”

Brett McNeill · The Seed of the Woman · Esther 2:1-23 · Oct 13, 2025 · Watch this part (16:10) →
