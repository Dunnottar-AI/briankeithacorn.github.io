What happens when we die?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“And the Lord had to prepare his people for that because death is necessary if God is going to rescue his people from sin and death. And that's why Jesus came. That's why he was born. He didn't come into this world because he was bored. He didn't come into this world for a vacation. He came into this world to die for us. He couldn't come into this world to save us and not die because the wages of sin is death. If we're going to be rescued from eternal death, someone had to pay the price. That's why he came.”

Brett McNeill · Born to Die · 2 Kings 4:8-37, Luke 1:26-35 · Dec 22, 2025 · Watch this part (25:55) →

“Death is the consequence of sin. That's what God threatened in the garden. The day you eat of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, you will surely die. And that means physical death, our bodies. It means spiritual death, the relationship that we have with God being broken, and it means eternal death in hell. That's the consequences for breaking God's law at just one point. That's the wages of sin. It's the just punishment. In our sin, we are under God's wrath and condemnation. And only judgment awaits. And that judgment is defined as death.”

Brett McNeill · First-Fruits · 1 Corinthians 15:12-23 · Apr 20, 2026 · Watch this part (11:32) →

“And the meaning was unmistakable. God was telling Abraham, "If I have to die to keep my promises, I will." And that's not bravado. Because in order to show mercy, God would have to suffer the consequences that Abraham's sins deserved. And the wages of sin is death. In order to to show Adam, it's not Adam, Abraham mercy, God would have to die. In order to do that, he would have to become man because deity can't die. And so at this point in Genesis 15, God is promising the incarnation that he would become man in order to suffer death, in order to keep the promise of salvation that Abraham was trusting that day.”

Brett McNeill · Breaking Bread with Sinners · Romans 4:1-12 · Sep 14, 2026 · Watch this part (15:12) →

“Especially after the fall, right? Creation is so full of decay. It's full of misery and sin. God surely can't wait to burn everything up as we read in 2 Peter 3, where we're told that the heavens will pass away with a roar and the heavenly bodies will be burned up and dissolved and the heavens will be set on fire and dissolved and the heavenly bodies will melt as they burn. But if God didn't think it was good for the heavens and earth to remain and to be here right now, guess what, guys? They wouldn't exist. They would be gone. It would have already happened. God thought it was good to create them and he still thinks it's good that everything exists.”

Andrew Davis · A World of Love · Genesis 1:1, 1 John 4:7-17 · Jul 27, 2026 · Watch this part (12:04) →
