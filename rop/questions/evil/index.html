Why does God allow evil?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“It serves as a symbol of the broader apparent evil in the world. An evil which is widespread. An evil that will with subtrifuge attack the righteous and steal from them. An evil that hates and mocks the good. An evil with murderous intent that will stop at nothing to get what it wants. This is the evil that scoffers see and ask, "Where is God? Where is the omnipotent and allloving God of yours? Why hasn't he stopped this?" And on its face, those are fair questions.”

David Chilton · The Problem of Evil · Psalm 11 · Aug 31, 2026 · Watch this part (10:29) →

“This is what Satan did on the cross. But God promised Satan that would not be the end. And that campaign would come back on his own head and that head would be crushed and he would suffer a death blow. What God what Satan intends for evil, God would turn into his destruction. And this is exactly how our passage describes Hmon's fall in verse 25. His evil plan returned on his own head. That's why they named it Pum to remember Hmon's plot. The name that Revelation gives, the marriage supper of the lamb drinks in the same delicious irony. Jesus suffered on the cross as a sacrificial lamb where Satan thought he would gain victory.”

Brett McNeill · The Last Feast · Esther 9:16-32, Revelation 19:1-10 · Dec 15, 2025 · Watch this part (21:37) →

“Why do we pray for people with chronic health issues every Sunday at church? Because it can be hard to keep your focus on eternal things when you are so laid down with temporal suffering. And the most important thing your friends can pray for is that your faith would not just endure but grow in the midst of your hardship because suffering can grow your faith as well. And Paul serves as a wonderful example of this. he suffered some from some ailment of the eyes. We're not sure exactly what it was.”

Brett McNeill · Prayer · Philippians 4:4-8, James 5:13-18 · Oct 5, 2026 · Watch this part (26:10) →

“He's been left for dead and yet survived. He's been imprisoned and he's done all of this for the sake of the gospel. He even sees his physical ailments a as part of this. He talks about this affliction that he has with his eyes as part of his suffering for Christ. He sees the pain he has endured from natural disasters as part of suffering for the gospel. He's he's endured shipwrecks. He spent a couple nights a drift in the middle of the sea. Paul's entire ministry has been marked by persecution, by pain, and by suffering.”

Brett McNeill · Christ in You · Colossians 1:24 - 2:5 · Mar 15, 2026 · Watch this part (10:44) →
