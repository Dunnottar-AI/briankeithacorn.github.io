What does God say about family?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“That our parents have never been there. Parents have been where their children are. The warnings and the rules that they give are actually because our parents understand us very well. The second lie that children believe is that saying no to you means your parents don't love you. The opposite is true. God often tells us no because he loves us. And the same is true with our earthly parents. Another lie is that agreeing with you or not agreeing with you means that they haven't heard you.”

Brett McNeill · The Cross in the Family · Colossians 3:20-21 · May 18, 2026 · Watch this part (19:19) →

“Maybe educating them. For others, their vocation is being a student at least for a season. Children have jobs at home. Hopefully if you somebody comes over and says, "What's your job?" They can list off some things that they contribute to the family. In other words, there is something in this passage for everyone. And those words in verse 23, "Whatever you do," if they sound familiar, if they if they're echoing something, it's intentional. They're echoing verse 17 that we looked at a few weeks back. "Whatever you do, in word or in deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him." That echo is intentional because this passage and the and the last two that we've looked at on husbands and wives and parents and children and now masters and servants are always to”

“You may remember some adherence of this group came and tried to trick Jesus with a scenario about this woman who had married a man and they had no children and according to Jewish law it was the job of the man's brother then to marry the wife to give his deceased brother offspring heirs for his property and so on and so the Sadducees tell Jesus this woman marries this first brother and they have no children so she marries the second brother they have no children so She marries the third brother and they have no children. On and on through seven brothers. Whose wife? They ask will she be in the resurrection?”

Brett McNeill · The Heart of it All · 1 Corinthians 15:1-14 · Apr 13, 2026 · Watch this part (8:10) →

“We love to have God's children in worship. We really do. But it means teaching them what worship is. Occasional outbursts are going to be happen. And that just it's a joy. We remember that they're here. We get it. But if you ask them to quiet down and they don't, it's respectful to step out, use the training room. It's a process. It takes time. We are always going to welcome children to worship with us and we will always provide ways to help parents while they train them because children are important to us. And they're not just important to us, they are important to God. That's why he has us bring them for baptism.”
