Where does my help come from?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“He'd be waiting forever because we have no hope, no ability, nothing to offer. And so we'd be with no hope, no rescue, and no salvation. Our help comes from heaven, comes down to earth, down to us. He didn't abandon heaven. He didn't look at the earth and say, "Well, that's better. Why don't we all just make our home there?" Remember, we're pilgrims. We're sojourners. Heaven is our home. He came down to rescue us.”

Brett McNeill · From Where Does My Help Come? · Psalm 121 · Jun 15, 2026 · Watch this part (24:00) →

“Jesus was baptized on the cross to rescue us. Our only hope is in one who would be born of a woman and suffer great injury but find ultimate victory. Jesus Christ alone is Simon and Jose's hope. They have no hope outside of him. Our prayer is that each comes to a place where they place their faith in him alone for their salvation. That is our hope. After their baptisms, we'll have a second opportunity to see these truths driven home in the Lord's supper, which reminds us that our hope is as it has been since the fall.”

Brett McNeill · It Started in a Garden · Esther 1:1-22 · Oct 6, 2025 · Watch this part (33:32) →

“We all need help in this world. We need guidance. We need leaders who can speak into our lives with God's word and help us to grow. When we fall into complacency, when we're stuck in conflict and can't can't find resolution our own, we need someone to come along and help. We're sheep and sheep need shepherds, but God has not left us without shepherds. Your elders are here to watch over you and to guide you and to help you. That's what they have been called to by Jesus.”

Brett McNeill · Jesus is King! · 1 Peter 5:1-4 · Jan 26, 2026 · Watch this part (20:12) →

“these Psalms, as we've been seeing, underscored the reality that God's people are pilgrims and sojourners in this world, never truly at home until they are in heaven with their God. And I've mentioned before that these Psalms come in groups of three. And and there's this pattern to these triplets. The first in the group of three is a cry for help. It's a cry of distress. Act really. And then And then the second one is the more specific request of help. So, the first one says, "Here's my problem." The second one says, "Please help me." And then the third one is a Psalm of confidence in the presence of God. We've already done three.”

Brett McNeill · A Servant's Prayer · Psalm 123 · Jul 6, 2026 · Watch this part (6:06) →
