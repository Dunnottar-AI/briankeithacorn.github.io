How can I welcome others?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“What is hospitality? It's simply love toward others. Most especially love toward those who are new to us. The word actually means to love a stranger. You see, being hospitable makes up that larger expression of love. All of life is love toward God. All of life is love toward others. And oh father, as you've given you've given yourself to me, you've welcomed me. Oh Lord, may I indeed be a mirror, may I reflect your love to others, loving those who are new, loving those I don't know all that well, loving those who are strangers.”

Mark Sumpter · Pursue Hospitality · Romans 12:1-13 · Feb 16, 2026 · Watch this part (13:37) →

“So the question is, if we're living in that moment between these restorations, between these resurre resurrections, what does this psalm tell us life looks like as we wait in this world, awaiting that day? That's what verses five and six are about as they describe sewing in tears. to understand these two verses and the Christian life, we need to accept that there is a very definite pattern in scripture. There are two options when it comes to this life and to eternity. You are either a citizen of this world and a stranger to heaven or you are a citizen of heaven and a stranger to this world.”

Brett McNeill · Sorrows Turned to Joy · Psalm 126 · Aug 10, 2026 · Watch this part (21:04) →

“They cannot be insubordinate, unable to submit to others. Men who always talk, never listen, never take counsel, are not leadership material. They have to be gentle, not quick-tempered. They must be hospitable. Now, hospitality doesn't mean they have their close friends over often. It's how they treat other people, strangers, others. It carries over into how they talk.”

Brett McNeill · Jesus is King! · 1 Peter 5:1-4 · Jan 26, 2026 · Watch this part (24:48) →

“Righteous, humble, indestructible, willing to suffer for his people. one whose life would be threatened on a tree, but who would emerge victorious over his enemy. And so on the third day, everything changed. Esther, who had been so afraid to come into her king's presence, was welcomed in. Far from being unwelcome, the king delighted in her. He offered to share the fullness of his kingdom with her and give her anything she asked for. She asked one thing. deliverance from her enemy who sought her destruction. I mean, isn't that the one thing we need to be delivered from Satan's claim on us?”

Brett McNeill · The Gospel According to Esther · Esther 10:1-3 · Dec 29, 2025 · Watch this part (16:17) →
