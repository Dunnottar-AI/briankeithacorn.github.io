What is humility?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“Humility wants others to feel heard. Pride speaks. Humility listens. Pride acts. Humility prays. Pride talks about self. Humility asks questions about others. Pride curates an image. Humility speaks vulnerably. Pride is busy. Humility has time for others. Pride demands respect. Humility shows respect.”

Brett McNeill · Humility · Psalm 124 · Jul 20, 2026 · Watch this part (11:07) →

“To seek forgiveness when you blow it, and to freely grant forgiveness when they do. God is at work in you during your early years. He's shaping you, and what you make of that time is largely dependent on how you respond, on your heart, and what you do with the parents that God has given you. Obedience is hard. It is humbling and it is good and it is pleasing to the Lord. Humility is what Jesus is calling you to and humility is what true maturity looks like.”

Brett McNeill · The Cross in the Family · Colossians 3:20-21 · May 18, 2026 · Watch this part (22:37) →

“And the proud in verse four refer to those who are in power. The Bible acknowledges that in this world it is often the wicked who prosper and the righteous who suffer. That is a recurring theme in the Psalms, Psalm 10, 37, 73. Job talks about it regularly. The prophets talk about it. That doesn't mean that God is blessing them. It just means that he allows them to explore their sinfulness for a time, all the while storing up judgment for themselves. But make no mistake, God opposes the proud. They are not rooted in reality. They think that the way things are, they will always be.”

Brett McNeill · A Servant's Prayer · Psalm 123 · Jul 6, 2026 · Watch this part (12:45) →

“We will never find peace. If our worship is patterned after this world, it will not offer a refuge from this world. You cannot look to Hollywood to learn how to do worship and think that you will find peace. It is only when we stand before God and allow him to confront our pride and our disorder and our imbalance that we will begin to find peace. Biblical worship is and it has always been robust and well-ordered. It all of its parts working together like a well-oiled machine to humble us, to confront us, and to draw our focus off of ourselves and onto Christ, off of this world and onto our heavenly home, away from false comforts and false gods to the only true source”

Brett McNeill · Peace in Jerusalem · Psalm 122 · Jun 29, 2026 · Watch this part (23:55) →
