Where can I find peace?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“We want peace with our families. We want peace with our past. the older we get, I think the more we learn that the that peace like this is really only possible where there's grace. The simple reality is we have all been let down by those we love. And we have all let those we love down. We have hurt the people who matter to us the most. And and if peace was dependent upon how good we are, how well we act or behave, if the degree of peace we experience depends on how righteous we are, none of us would ever know true peace.”

Brett McNeill · Jesus, Our Peace · Ephesians 2:11-22 · Aug 24, 2026 · Watch this part (4:38) →

“of comfort, the God of heaven. And it's that reality that helps us understand the prayer that we find in the last four verses. Let us read verses 6 through 9. Pray for the peace of Jerusalem. May they be secure who love you. Peace within your walls and security within your towers. For my brothers and companions' sake, I will say, "Peace be within you. For the sake of the house of the Lord our God, I will seek your good." Verses 6 and 7, they pray for peace and security within Jerusalem.”

Brett McNeill · Peace in Jerusalem · Psalm 122 · Jun 29, 2026 · Watch this part (24:56) →

“It's being at peace with reality. It's being at peace with who you were made to be. It's being at peace with who made you. To be at peace with God and his word and his authority and his claim on all things, including you. You know the Hebrew word shalom. It's only possible when you stop resisting your creator and you instead surrender to him. As long as you're fighting against him, as long as you're waring against his way and his authority and his word, there is no peace. It only when you surrender that there's peace.”

Brett McNeill · The First Step · Psalm 120 · Jun 8, 2026 · Watch this part (21:10) →

“And so these truths are they're familiar to most of us. We we heard them in our declaration of pardon today. And we know that a lot of these are declared to us in the first half of Ephesians 2. Sadly, we're we're far less familiar with the second half of Ephe of Ephesians 2. Paul doesn't stop when he says, "For by grace you have been saved through faith." He he doesn't say, "You have peace with God." That's great. There's nothing more to say. As soon as he says that, he then says, "He himself is our peace, who made us both one, and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility, so that he might create in himself one new man in place of the two, so making peace, and might reconcile us both to God in one body through the cross, thereby killing the hostility." In other words, when you have peace with Jesus,”

Brett McNeill · Fellowship · 1 Corinthians 12:12-27, Matthew 22:34-40 · Oct 12, 2026 · Watch this part (21:46) →
