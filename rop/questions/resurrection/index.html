Did Jesus really rise from the dead?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“Where does the Old Testament say that the Messiah would die and be raised on the third day?" And the answer is a bit complicated because in one sense, nowhere does the Old Testament simply and clearly say the old that the Messiah will come, be put to death in Jerusalem, and die, and raise on the third day. But on the other hand, there are a host of passages, many passages that show that healing comes only through death and resurrection. And that the day of healing, the day of resurrection is always, always, always the third day.”

Brett McNeill · The Risen Physician · Hosea 6:1-10; Matthew 9:1-13 · Apr 6, 2026 · Watch this part (7:29) →

“And so resurrection is the only word that could be fitting for such an event. If Jesus was resuscitated but not resurrected, the story would be very different. And that's what the beginning of our passage is about, verses 12 through 19. It's about what matters. These verses focus on the implications of denying the resurrection. Why the resurrection matters. Now again, there were some in the church in Corinth who were arguing that there was no resurrection from the dead, that when you die, that's it.”

Brett McNeill · First-Fruits · 1 Corinthians 15:12-23 · Apr 20, 2026 · Watch this part (18:59) →

“The scriptures were clear that death could not have the final word and that the third day is the day of resurrection. And we have more passages to cover in the years to come. But like the resurrection, I sorry, like the crucifixion, the resurrection is not a bare event. It it too has a purpose. In another letter, Paul says it this way. Jesus was raised for our justification. the resurrection was more than a reversal of death. It was that, but so much more. It was the declaration or the verdict in the heavenly courts.”

Brett McNeill · The Heart of it All · 1 Corinthians 15:1-14 · Apr 13, 2026 · Watch this part (22:30) →

“May the waters of life once more course through our lives. And so we ask, is there a parallel for this as well for us? Are we meant to see the resurrection of Jesus and dare to hope that our resurrection will follow? And the answer is absolutely. That's the language that runs through 1 Corinthians 15. Jesus twice is called the first fruits of the resurrection. in verses 20 and 23. And we've talked about this before. It's an agricultural term. The first fruits are a part the first part of the harvest, a foretaste of what is to follow.”

Brett McNeill · Sorrows Turned to Joy · Psalm 126 · Aug 10, 2026 · Watch this part (19:35) →
