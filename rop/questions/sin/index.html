What is sin?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“He he quotes Genesis 15:6. Abraham believed God and it was counted to him as righteousness. In other words, Paul's saying, remember Abraham was a sinner. He was not righteous before God on his own. He was not good enough. And Genesis records some of those sins for us. Now, don't get me wrong. He was more righteous than I am for sure. But before God, he was a sinner in need of forgiveness. So, what was Abraham's hope? His hope was that God had promised to show him mercy and grace and forgiveness.”

Brett McNeill · Breaking Bread with Sinners · Romans 4:1-12 · Sep 14, 2026 · Watch this part (12:50) →

“They were to offer sacrifices. Central to the place where God and his people would meet was the altar. On the altar, a substitute would be offered in the place of the sinner. Because the wages of sin is death, physical, spiritual, and eternal death. Someone needs to die for sin. And on the altar, a bull or a lamb or a goat or something else would be offered in the place of the sinner. And the fire would consume the sacrifice and not the sinner himself. That's how a holy God can allow sinners to enter into his presence. through sacrifice, through substitution, through the altar.”

Brett McNeill · Holy Ground · Hebrews 12:18-24 · Jan 5, 2026 · Watch this part (17:53) →

“It's not aging and dying. Our greatest problem, every single one of us, is the sin within our own hearts because of God's law. Sin's not there because of our God's law, but because of God's law, our sin is our greatest problem. The Bible says that the power of sin is the law. And what it means is this. Sin can do nothing if there's no law, no covenant, no contract that states expectations and consequences for breaking it. The reason sin has power over us is because of that law that God gave Adam and Eve that said, "The day you break my commandment, you will surely die.”

Brett McNeill · The New Covenant · Esther 8:3-10 · Nov 17, 2025 · Watch this part (9:21) →

“And then he sent them to Hosea to tell them how that he would suffer all that we deserved in our place, that on the cross he would take our sin, our shame, our guilt, and he would bear it in our place. And he would do this because it was the only way, the only way for forgiveness, the only way out to heal all sinners. And that's his goal. To tell us how our sins are forgiven. But how? How could we know? How could we be sure that he has the authority to forgive sins? How How did the paralytic know that his sins were forgiven?”

Brett McNeill · The Risen Physician · Hosea 6:1-10; Matthew 9:1-13 · Apr 6, 2026 · Watch this part (27:37) →
