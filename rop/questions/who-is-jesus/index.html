Who is Jesus?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“Naomi and Ruth are both widows. Their husbands have died. And I'm I'm sure you mostly know that story. And I just want to reflect on some of the things, largely speaking, that the book of Ruth tells us about Christ's deity in the broader context. And so, what I want to drive home this morning is this. In Jesus' earthly ministry, he behaves exactly like the God of Israel does throughout all the Old Testament and specifically in the book of Ruth. And by doing that, he shows us that he is the God of Israel. Now, that's not typically the sort of argument that's brought forth to when we discuss his deity, the fact that he's God.”

Brett McNeill · The Deity of Christ · Exodus 34:5-9; John 1:14-18 · Jul 13, 2026 · Watch this part (5:45) →

“And the meaning was unmistakable. God was telling Abraham, "If I have to die to keep my promises, I will." And that's not bravado. Because in order to show mercy, God would have to suffer the consequences that Abraham's sins deserved. And the wages of sin is death. In order to to show Adam, it's not Adam, Abraham mercy, God would have to die. In order to do that, he would have to become man because deity can't die. And so at this point in Genesis 15, God is promising the incarnation that he would become man in order to suffer death, in order to keep the promise of salvation that Abraham was trusting that day.”

Brett McNeill · Breaking Bread with Sinners · Romans 4:1-12 · Sep 14, 2026 · Watch this part (15:12) →

“This is the verse the apostle John had in mind when he said, "The word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only son of God from the father, full of grace and truth. That's that's abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness. And so John says, "No one has ever seen God, the only God of the Father's side. He has made him known." And this is what Colossians 1 was saying when it said Jesus is the image of the invisible God. He shows us who God is. He teaches us what that name represents. And he does that by showing love and compassion, by showing mercy and forgiving sins even at great cost to himself.”

Brett McNeill · Put on Christ · Colossians 3:5-17 · May 4, 2026 · Watch this part (28:31) →

“And yet, we have to ask, "Okay, what does that mean?" And that's what we want to look at today. Our passage is one of the most robust passages in the Bible about who Jesus is. It deals with His deity, the fact that He is the eternal, uncreated God who created the world. It addresses His incarnation, that the creator entered into the creation as man, and it tells us why He entered into the creation, to reconcile sinful people to a holy God. And these things teach us about who our God is, and that's the point. And so, what we want to see this morning from this passage of scripture in Colossians is this, in Jesus we see who God is.”

Brett McNeill · The Image of the Invisible God · Colossians 1:15-23 · Mar 9, 2026 · Watch this part (5:43) →
