Does God care about my work?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“And then finally, at the end, what we'll see is how Paul applies this to the workplace, to our jobs. What we're going to What we'll find is that everyone has a job to do, and that job is an opportunity to take up your cross in service to the Lord whether you are in authority or under authority. And so, that's what we want to see today. As we saw in the our reading in the confession, Adam had a job to do when he was created. if we look at Genesis 2, God tells us that Adam's job consisted of mainly two things. He was to work the garden, and he was to guard the garden.”

Brett McNeill · The Cross at Work · Colossians 3:22-4:1 · May 25, 2026 · Watch this part (10:44) →

“In other words, we are servants who look not just to a master, but the great master, the master of masters, the king of kings and the lord of lords. The only master truly worthy of our devotion and our loyalty. We're not looking for autonomy, we're looking for the right master. And the reason he is worthy of our devotion is not just because he is the master of masters, but because he is also the servant of servants. Going back to that scenario that I opened with it at the beginning, you being a doorman in the White House, I want you to imagine now that this president, before he had been president, had served in the military, been shot down behind enemy lines, and had been a POW for some time.”

Brett McNeill · A Servant's Prayer · Psalm 123 · Jul 6, 2026 · Watch this part (22:39) →

“That's what we want to see this morning. And to do that, we're going to need to see how much of this language in our psalm actually goes back to creation, to Genesis 1 and two and three, back to the Garden of Eden and what Adam and Eve were called to do. And then we'll be able to see how that language is later picked up with Solomon, but eventually with Jesus himself and how he takes up this language to describe his work of building a new house in the church and that he invites us to enter into and join him in that work but in dependence upon him and not ourselves. So the three things that we see picked up again in this psalm from everyday life are work, the work of building, protection, the work of guarding, and family, children, having children.”

Brett McNeill · Unless the Lord Builds the House · Psalm 127 · Aug 17, 2026 · Watch this part (6:52) →

“First his family and all he possessed and then his flesh. And you wonder why. Because as you read the book of Job, nothing but pain and heartache follow. And confusion fills the pages of Job much as confusion fills the city of Susa in our in our passage. But as you get to the end of the book of Job, you realize God had a purpose. Satan thought he was winning, but he was being played. God had a purpose for Job, and Satan was just the means he used to get there.”

Brett McNeill · The Seed of the Serpent · Esther 3:1-15 · Oct 20, 2025 · Watch this part (33:16) →
