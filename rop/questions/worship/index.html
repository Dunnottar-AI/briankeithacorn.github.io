What is worship?

Here is what was said about this in our sermons. These are the preachers' own words. Tap a clip to hear it.

“And that's what we want to talk about today. What I hope to show you as we look at a few passages in God's word is that reverent worship acknowledges that God is holy and we are sinful and it finds comfort in his saving grace. That's what we want to look at today. I should probably start by defining reverence. Says let us offer to God acceptable worship with reverence and all. Okay. So what is reverence? Reverence is about fear, godly fear. It's about understanding that God is God and that he is holy and that he cares about his holiness and that we will all stand before him on the last day.”

Brett McNeill · Reverent Worship · Hebrews 12:25-29 · Feb 2, 2026 · Watch this part (4:43) →

“God has given us worship for our benefit, not because he's needy. But that's very different from saying that worship is about us or centers on us. Worship that is about us or centered on us is not acceptable to the God who shares his glory with no other. As we heard in our call to worship this morning, worship that is man-c centered is idolatry. It's what drove the golden calf. It's what motivated Naab and Abaiu. And it's what emboldened Kora and his associates. Our our worship, our praise. It's an it's a gift, an offering to God.”

Brett McNeill · Acceptable Worship · Hebrews 12:25-29 · Jan 19, 2026 · Watch this part (23:26) →

“If there's acceptable worship, there's also unacceptable worship. Scripture is full of warnings against inventing our own ways to worship. How can God ever wrestle with us if all we do is protect ourselves from anything that makes us uncomfortable? After talking about acceptable worship, we spent a week talking about what it means to offer reverent worship. Reverence is about fear, appropriate godly fear. Recognizing that God is God and that we are not.”

Brett McNeill · Sunday's Shadow · Luke 11:1-3 · Feb 23, 2026 · Watch this part (10:43) →

“The hour is coming and is now here when true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and truth." Jesus is not saying Deuteronomy 12 is wrong. We can worship wherever we want. He's not saying that the place of worship doesn't matter. And he's not saying that we are free to worship God anywhere. He's saying that with his coming, a change is taking place. God is moving his place of worship. I think we instinctively get that's coming. Even when Solomon dedicated his temple, he says, "How can the heavens contain God, much less this house that I have built?" God's home is heaven.”

Brett McNeill · Holy Ground · Hebrews 12:18-24 · Jan 5, 2026 · Watch this part (22:29) →
